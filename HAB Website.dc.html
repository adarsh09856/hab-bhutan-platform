About us

A network built for the artisans, not the middlemen

HAB is dedicated to establishing a strong network for Bhutanese artisans that guarantees fair compensation for their handcrafted products and improved market accessibility. The organization not only invests in training and resources to enhance the quality of handmade crafts, but also advocates for the sector through dialogue with policymakers.

HAB plays a critical role in the Bhutanese handicraft industry, with a network of 7,500 micro and small enterprises across the country — women-led (5,250) and men-led (2,250), formal and informal — and 195 affiliated stores exhibiting more than 100 unique handcrafted products.

Programmes

Eleven objects, one mandate

HAB operates as the national apex Public Benefit Organisation for Bhutan's handicrafts sector, advancing the productive, economic, cultural and social well-being of all actors across the handicrafts value chain.

Every programme runs against one or more of the objects set out in Article 3.2 of the Articles of Association. Activity outside those objects is ultra vires and of no effect.

Governing principles

Public Benefit · Integrity · Inclusivity · Cultural Stewardship · Compliance · Independence

Constituted under the Civil Society Organizations Act of Bhutan 2007 (as amended 2022). National scope across all twenty dzongkhags. Non-political by constitution.

Programme types

The objects are construed broadly: each is a standing programme area, not a fixed project. Article 3.2 (a)–(k).

Who we are

A pioneer centre for Bhutanese handicrafts

Handicrafts Association of Bhutan (HAB) was established in 2005 and got formally registered as Civil Society Organization in 2011 under the CSO act of Bhutan 2007 as a pioneer centre for the promotion of vibrant and sustainable Bhutanese handicrafts.

{{ af.k }}

Vision

Towards a vibrant & sustainable handicrafts sector

A Bhutan where the thirteen crafts remain in daily practice, and where making them is a livelihood a young person would choose.

Mission

Promoting sustainability, inclusiveness and resilience

HAB supports local artisans by providing resources, training and policy interventions to improve their skills and increase their chances of success in local communities and the tourism industry.

Objectives

What we set out to do

Six objectives carried in the strategic plan 2026–2030, against which the Board reviews performance each year.

Governance

How HAB is governed

Authority runs from the members upward: the General Assembly elects the Board, the Board appoints and oversees the Executive Director, and the secretariat delivers. Committees sit between Board and secretariat for finance, ethics and procurement.
